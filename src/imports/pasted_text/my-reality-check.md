Create a new **My Reality** activity that feels like a short, fun social-media reality check rather than a school quiz.

### Intro screen

Title:

**My Reality**

Main heading:

**How well do you know your feed? 👀**

Subtext:

**10 quick reality checks. See how much your feed might be leaving out.**

Add a clear **Start** button.

### Overall quiz design

* Show **one question per screen**.
* Add a simple progress indicator at the top, such as **3/10** with a playful progress bar or 10 small icons/tiles that fill in as users move through the activity.
* Keep the design colorful, casual, and teen-friendly.
* Avoid making it feel like a test or school assignment.
* Do not use points, rankings, streaks, or a leaderboard.
* Mix up the interaction style so every question does not look exactly the same.
* Use mock social-media posts/photos on some questions when possible.

### Question types

Use a mix of:

* Multiple-choice questions with large tappable answer cards
* True/false questions with two large choices
* “Pick the best take” scenario questions
* A visual number-guess question
* Questions built around a mock social-media post or feed

After every answer, show immediate feedback before moving to the next question.

If correct, use something simple like:

**Yep 👀**

If incorrect:

**Not quite 👀**

Then show a short 1–2 sentence explanation of the reality check and a **Next** button.

### Use these 10 questions

**1. How many teens say social media sometimes makes them feel pressure to look their best or come across a certain way?**

A. About 1 in 5
B. About 2 in 5
C. About 4 in 5
D. Pretty much everyone

Correct answer: **About 4 in 5**

For this question, make the answer choices visual using groups of 5 person icons instead of only text.

---

**2. Besides looks, what else can people feel pressure about online?**

A. Having a fun social life
B. Doing well or achieving things
C. Always being there for friends
D. All of these

Correct answer: **All of these**

---

**3. Your friend posts pics from an amazing weekend. What’s probably the most accurate way to think about it?**

A. Their weekend was probably way better than yours
B. If the pics are real, that’s basically what their whole weekend was like
C. The pics can be totally real and still leave out a lot
D. People usually post the good and bad stuff pretty evenly

Correct answer: **The pics can be totally real and still leave out a lot**

For this question, show a realistic mock social-media post with several fun weekend photos.

---

**4. True or false?**

**Once you know social media is filtered and curated, it shouldn’t really affect how you compare yourself anymore.**

Correct answer: **False**

Use two large **True / False** buttons.

---

**5. Who do people compare themselves to online?**

A. Mostly celebrities
B. Mostly influencers
C. Mostly people they know
D. It can be friends, classmates, influencers, celebrities, or basically anyone

Correct answer: **It can be basically anyone**

---

**6. Your feed suddenly feels like everyone is going to the gym. What does that actually tell you?**

A. Most people your age are working out all the time
B. Fitness must be getting way more popular
C. Your friends are more active than you
D. Not much. Your feed is partly shaped by what you click on and interact with

Correct answer: **D**

For this question, show a mock feed filled with gym/fitness posts.

---

**7. Someone posts a selfie and gets a ton of likes. What does that tell you about how they feel about themselves?**

A. They’re probably really confident
B. They probably love how they look
C. They probably have high self-esteem
D. You really can’t tell

Correct answer: **You really can’t tell**

---

**8. In one study, teens and young adults cut their social media use by about half for a few weeks. What happened?**

A. Nothing really changed
B. They felt more left out
C. They felt better about their appearance and weight
D. They got more anxious

Correct answer: **They felt better about their appearance and weight**

Make this feel like a surprising **Did you know?** question.

---

**9. You think, “Everyone is always hanging out and I’m the only one sitting at home.” What are you really comparing?**

A. Your best moments to their best moments
B. Your online life to their offline life
C. Your whole life to the moments they chose to post
D. You’re not really comparing anything

Correct answer: **Your whole life to the moments they chose to post**

---

**10. Which of these is closest to the truth about social media and mental health?**

A. Social media is bad for everyone
B. Social media doesn’t really affect mental health
C. More time online always means worse mental health
D. It can help or hurt depending on what you see, what you do, and how it affects you

Correct answer: **D**

### Final screen

Do not show a numerical score.

Instead show:

**Reality Check Complete ✨**

Then highlight three takeaways:

**Real doesn’t always mean the whole story.**

**Your feed isn’t everyone’s reality.**

**Knowing something is curated doesn’t always stop comparison.**

Add a **Done** button.

Keep the overall experience quick, visually varied, playful, and similar in feel to apps like Kahoot or Duolingo, while still matching the visual style of the rest of this app.
